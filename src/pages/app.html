The seven web tools stay free and unlimited either way. Pro adds work the browser tools do not do; it does not fence off what is already free.

Why the app is free

Running it costs us nothing per user, because your phone does the work. It stays free while we are new and finding out what people actually need from it.

What we cannot sell

Your documents. They are never on our machines, so there is no data business here even if we wanted one. That is the same reason nothing but AI summaries asks you to sign in.

What Pro will pay for

The parts that do cost money: AI summaries, the heavier OCR work, and keeping this maintained. Paid once, not rented — a PDF tool gets used a few times a year, and every competitor bills monthly for it. You will hear about it from the app rather than from a banner here.

Why summaries are only ever in the app

Summarising means sending the text of your document to Google's Gemini API, and it needs an account to meter. Both of those are fine in an app you chose to install. Neither is fine on the website, whose whole argument is that nothing leaves your device — and which prints a live count of bytes sent at the bottom of every page so you can check that claim yourself.

Putting summaries on the web would break the one promise the site makes checkable, in the exact place it is checkable, and would need sign-in on a site whose position is that it has none. So they are not coming to the web. That is a decision, not a roadmap gap.

What the app sends, and what it does not

The app is not silent in the way this website is, and it would be dishonest to imply otherwise on a page selling it.

  • Your PDFs and photos stay on the phone. Every editing tool runs locally.
  • It contains Firebase Analytics and Crashlytics, which report feature usage and crashes.
  • AI summarise sends the extracted text of a document to Google's Gemini API. It is the only feature that does, and the only one needing an account.
  • It contains no advertising SDK, and asks for neither storage nor camera permission.

The privacy page covers both surfaces in full.
